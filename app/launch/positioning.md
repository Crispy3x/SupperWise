# Supperwise · positioning (2026-11-11)

## The shelf: "budget meal planner", US
Read with `launch/research/search-budget-meal-planner.md` and `shelf-budget-meal-planner/shelf.png`.

Top results: TapCook (7.4K ratings, 2026), Whipp (758), Potto (2.4K), eMeals (52.8K, 2013), NeatEat (1.5K), mise (4.9K), MealPrepPro (12.2K), HelloFresh. Five of the top six launched in 2026.

**What every one of them looks like:**
- a white or green ground
- a phone in a frame
- a 2–4 word headline: "Meal plan in seconds", "Set your own budget", "Stress-free recipes"
- food photos in rounded cards
- a budget slider

Green and orange dominate.

**What nobody shows:**
- the bill priced by the pack you actually buy
- a household with different rules on one plate
- the price before the paywall

**Watch:** "Supper Tab: Meal Planner" (0 ratings, new). It uses a dark red and cream look with serif headlines, and its name starts with "Supper". Our panels must not lean on tomato red plus a serif alone; lead with the weekday colour system, several colours across the set.

## What the competition gets wrong
From 50 recent reviews of the five leaders, read in step 1 (factory/SPEC.md):
1. Diet, allergy and equipment settings ignored or too narrow: 12 of 50.
2. Bland, repetitive, vague or AI-made recipes: 11 of 50.
3. A paywall or trial that feels like a trick: 10 of 50.
4. A bill that doesn't match the store, priced by the spoonful: 7 of 50.

Table stakes from the 5-star reviews: it takes the mental load off, saves money, and the swap works.

## The statement
- **For:** parents feeding 3–5 people on a weekly grocery budget, where not everyone eats the same thing.
- **Who:** at 5pm, deciding again, then finding out the damage at the register.
- **Instead of:** a meal-kit subscription, a generic planner that ignores the vegetarian at the table, or a notes app and hope.
- **Supperwise is:** a weekly dinner planner with a priced grocery list.
- **That:** gives the whole family one week of dinners that fits the budget, before you shop.
- **Because:**
  - It prices whole packs and buys shared packs once (Pricer.swift).
  - It applies each person's diet, allergies and spice level as hard rules, with a written vegetarian plate (Catalog.eligibleRecipes, tested).
  - It shows the first week before asking for money.
- **Enemy:** the 6pm "what's for dinner", and the $14 jar you bought for one teaspoon.
- **Proof:**
  - 19 written recipes with real prep steps
  - per-person rules
  - the whole-pack price with leftover notes
  - the first week kept free
  - no account, no data leaves the phone
- **Not for:** calorie or macro counting, meal-kit delivery, or people who want hundreds of recipes on day one.
